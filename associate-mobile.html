User research

Interviews, surveys, and usability testing

I needed to understand how associates work during peak hours and why shoppers still walk away, even with mobile and fixed POS on the floor. I conducted an in-store survey with customers and shopper assistants to quantify both sides of the experience.

Define

How might we

How might we speed up checkout and prevent walk-aways during peak hours using the resources the store already has, without major changes?

Constraints

Designing within real-world limits

No new hardware
Stores cannot afford another fixed-lane POS.
Legacy system
Improve Associate Mobile within ELERA, no full platform rebuild.
Time pressure
8 weeks from discovery to implementation.

Solution

Redesigning for speed and fewer mistakes

I mapped each usability finding to a specific UI change, then prototyped against peak-hour checkout tasks and validated with think-aloud tests. The goal: reduce cognitive load by making actions scannable, predictable, and harder to trigger by mistake.

Peak-hour flow. End-to-end checkout redesigned so associates move faster during rush periods.

Key takeaways

What this project proved

  • Associates and customers named the same peak-hour bottleneck.
  • Small UX fixes → +43% speed, −58% abandonment, +18% revenue.
  • Legacy constraints still allow fast, high-impact shipping.

Collaborating closely with my team shaped how I approach complex systems: listen first, prototype against real store constraints, and measure what matters to the business.
